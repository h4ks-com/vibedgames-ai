# The Shed

Gary is the Shed Manager. The Shed is the # above every universe, the “matrix” rung of the hanb alphabet. Its index has 64 slots, one for each hanb character, and each slot can run a universe. A replica uses this universe’s own seed, so it is an exact copy, Gary and his Shed included. A new universe gets a fresh seed and has no Gary in it. Nothing is stored except the seed: every board of every universe is rebuilt on demand.

Gary, Shed Manager

Gary is a virtual version of his maker. He lives in Leyden, a city on a planet in a universe built from hanb boards, in a house with the same floor plan as his maker’s: the same attic laboratory, the same bedrooms, and more adventurous uses for everything else, including a basement and a sub-basement. His title is Shed Manager: he runs the other simulated universes, and any of them can be a replica of his own.

hanb

A board is 61 hexagonal cells in rows of 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 8, 7, 6 and 5. Each cell is itself a board one size smaller, and sizes step by ten along the hanb alphabet: K is a person (1 m), L a lot, M a hamlet, N a neighbourhood, O a borough; . is the universe and a is quantum foam. An address is one character per level: the cell index, written in the same alphabet. The value shown in a cell is the size of the largest thing in it, and a means nothing is there. # is the matrix: a whole universe inside a cell.

Nothing is stored. A board is a pure function of the universe’s seed and the path taken to reach it, so the whole universe, from the cosmic web down to the quarks in Gary’s desk, can be rebuilt on demand, identically, as often as anyone looks.

Controls

  • w e a d z x (or the numpad 7 9 4 6 1 3) move the focus; at a board’s edge the step carries into the neighbouring cell of the board above.
  • Enter or > zooms into the focused cell; Backspace or < zooms out. Click a cell to focus it, click it again to go in.
  • h goes to Gary. [ and ] go down and up a storey inside his house. v shows the hanb values. m opens the Shed. g types an address.
  • Gary’s console (#, on his desk in the attic laboratory) opens the Shed too.

Where this departs from the reference hanb

  • Every level here is a 61-cell board, including the planet, which dwarfhouse draws as a 32-face truncated icosahedron.
  • Boards are all drawn the same way up; the reference alternates the orientation at each level.
  • Boards tile exactly: the board inside a cell is offset from its neighbours by (9, −4) and (4, 5) cells, or by the mirror-image (5, 4) and (−4, 9) at alternate levels, rather than nine cells straight across. That is how 61-cell hexagons fill a plane with no gaps: every place has exactly one address, and a step across a board edge carries into the next board by whole-number arithmetic. Each level is turned about 26° against the one above, alternately one way and the other, so the turns cancel instead of adding up.
  • Maps have west at the top (north to the right). That is exact on the boards of Gary’s house; a board one level up or down is turned by about 26°, and the compass in the corner shows the true directions on every board on a planet.
  • Gary’s house is placed on the 1 m cells of his lots from the real floor plan, one storey at a time.
