What runs. Each cell holds one of K states and has six neighbours on a hexagonal grid. Its next state is a function of eight inputs: its own state, the six neighbours, and its port. That is Cell-8. The port of every cell is read from the picture, so the picture decides which behaviour runs where.

The rule is never stored. With 16 states and 4 ports the full table would have about a billion entries. Each cell computes its own entry instead, as a hash of the seed and its inputs. A seed is therefore a whole rule; "New rule" picks another.

Two kinds of rule. Any table: the entry is a hash of all eight inputs, so the rule is a typical member of the whole Cell-8 rule space. Such rules have no structure in space and give coloured noise. Counting: a cell either keeps its state or moves on to the next one (after the last comes the first), and which it does depends only on its port and on two counts: how many neighbours are already in the next state, and how many are in its own. These are Cell-8 rules too, a small symmetric corner of the space, and many of them make waves, spirals and patches.

Stillness is the share of entries that leave a cell as it is: near 1 the board freezes, near 0 it boils. Port contrast gives the ports different stillness, so that bright and dark parts of the picture move at different rates; at 0 the ports differ only in which rule they run.

Port 0 holds still. With this on, cells at port 0 (the darkest part of the picture, or the first hue) never change, and act as walls. Turn it off and port 0 is a rule like the others.

Ports from the picture. By brightness, the cells are divided into equal-sized groups from darkest to lightest. By hue, the colour circle is cut into equal sectors, and cells with almost no colour go to port 0.

The grid wraps round at the edges. Everything is computed on the graphics card of this computer; no picture or setting leaves the page.
