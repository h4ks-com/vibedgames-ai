Rulebook (all 60 pages of it, abridged)

The real game (1978, SPI, Richard Berg): the most complex wargame ever mass-produced — 10 map sheets, ~3,000 counters, daily turns, tonnage-level fuel/water/ammo bookkeeping, a rule where Italian units need extra water to boil their pasta ration (yes, real — and if they don't get it, morale suffers), and an estimated 1,500 hours to finish. Best played by two teams of five.

This remake: the 1940–41 campaign in 20 weekly turns. Axis (Italy, 10th Army) tries to reach Alexandria; Allies (Western Desert Force) try to throw them back and take Bardia, Tobruk, and Benghazi.

  • Supply: units must sit within supply radius of an owned port/depot, and your ports must have capacity. Out of supply: −2 attack/def, lose a step every 2nd turn.
  • 🍝 The Pasta Rule (kept, obviously): Italian divisions need extra water above port supply. Not enough water → that division eats dry rations: −1 attack. Feed your men.
  • Movement: coastal road ½, desert 1, escarpment/Qattara 2. Entering a hex next to an enemy halts that unit.
  • Combat: all your adjacent units join in; odds on the CRT, die 1–6. +1 die if armor attacks infantry in open desert; −2 if defender holds a fortified port (Bardia, Tobruk, Sollum, Matruh, Alamein, Alexandria).
  • Air: 2 points/side/turn — interdict a hex (enemy movement there costs double) or ground-support one attack (+1 die).
  • Victory: Axis take Alexandria → decisive. Allies hold Bardia+Tobruk → major; add Benghazi → decisive. Turn 20: whoever holds Sidi Barrani gets the marginal win.
  • What we cut: 3,000 counters → 17 · searchlight rules · Time-on-Target artillery plotting · 600 trucks of water-boiling regulations · morale chits · you get the idea. The pasta stayed.

Hot-seat two-player, or play both sides and argue with yourself, as tradition demands.
